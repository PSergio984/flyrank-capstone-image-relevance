What is time but a tangle of memory and anticipation? This essay moves without pictures: Augustine, Husserl, and contemporary physics debate whether the present is an infinitesimal edge or an illusion. There are no cities, no forests, no animals, no vehicles, no food in this abstract argument. The honest system response is no confident match, with reasons explaining that no image category aligns with a purely conceptual meditation on temporality. We reference the arrow of entropy, the block universe, and why no mountain, city, or animal photograph could illustrate an argument that lives entirely in language and logic.
