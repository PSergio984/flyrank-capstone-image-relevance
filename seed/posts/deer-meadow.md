title: "White-Tailed Deer in the Meadow"
---

At dawn, white-tailed deer emerge to graze in meadows bordered by forest. Their reddish summer coats and white under-tails flash as they bound away. This piece observes the deer's crepuscular rhythm, its reliance on edge habitats, and how fawns use spotted camouflage to hide in tall grass during their first vulnerable weeks. We trace seasonal coat changes from red summer to gray winter, the role of whitetail social groups in predator detection, and why meadow-forest edges offer the richest forage at first light.
